Prize Board

Fourteen builds ranked by how much of a $45,500 pool each one can legitimately reach — and which one to actually build.

Aug 17 – Sept 3 · online + Santa Clara 544 registered 80+ judges Winners announced Sept 3, 3:30 PM PT
The rule that decides everything Teams can solve no challenges (build whatever you want) or can submit to as many challenges as they want. apiworld.co/hackathon

Judging runs in two rounds. Round 1 scores everything on three things: how much progress you made, whether it solves a real problem, and whether it could become a company. Round 2 is the sponsor round — each sponsor judges only the projects entered in their challenge and picks their own winners.

So your build isn't one bet, it's a portfolio. One project can enter six challenges, each a separate field with separate judges. Optimize for how many tracks a single coherent product can enter without any integration feeling bolted on — every sponsor scores integration depth, and a gratuitous API call reads as exactly what it is.

The call

Build Denial as the primary

It has the best combination of the four things that matter: a problem every judge has personally suffered, a demo with a number that lands like a punch, five tracks it enters without a single forced integration, and a startup story you can give in one sentence. Round 1 asks for progress, a real problem, and feasibility — all three are layups.

If you'd rather chase breadth, build Lookalike

It reaches $10,000 across six tracks, and it's the only concept here that makes name.com's $2,000 genuinely winnable — the domain API does real forensic work rather than getting name-dropped. Most entrants in that track will fake exactly what this one does for real.

Either way, run Mirror in parallel

Perfect Corp is the partner sponsor, pays $2,500 in straight cash, and both endpoints Mirror needs already exist. One person can build it in a day. It's the cheapest incremental expected value on the board — and it covers the visual-spectacle angle that a document tool structurally cannot. If you have a fifth person with a spare afternoon, point them at Spec to Suite: two $1,000 pots the field will leave uncontested.

Tactics that matter more than the idea

  • Get every API key before Aug 17. Nutrient, SerpApi and Foxit are instant and free. Perfect Corp, Doctavian and Xano have signup flows that can eat a day. You have five days of runway — spend them on credentials, not code.
  • Write a separate "where this sponsor does essential work" paragraph for every track you enter. Sponsors score integration depth and they read these. One generic writeup submitted six times is how strong projects lose thin fields.
  • Budget a full day for the demo video. Most tracks want 2–4 minutes; Perfect Corp wants 1–3. It's the highest-leverage hour of the hackathon and the thing every team under-invests in.
  • Build the audit view. Nutrient asks for deterministic, auditable output with a human in the loop. A visible diff/confidence/override panel is a feature you'd want anyway, and it turns one track from a maybe into a likely.
  • Frame Xano correctly. The track is "rebuild a SaaS tool you hate." Name the incumbent out loud in the writeup — the patient billing portal, the DMV site — and tell the build story they explicitly ask for.
  • Be in the room on Sept 2–3. Winners are announced live, several tracks require exit interviews, and 80+ judges circulating in person is not a channel to skip.
